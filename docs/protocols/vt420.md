# VT420

Left and right margins, and the column operations that ride on them.

- **Implementation:** `baud-core/src/ops.rs`
- **Tests:** `baud-core/tests/vt420/`
- **Sources:** `[[source: vt510-decslrm]]`, `[[source: vt510-decic]]`,
  `[[source: vt510-decdc]]`

## `CSI s` is ambiguous, and the protocol is what makes it so

`CSI s` is SCOSC normally and DECSLRM when DECLRMM (`?69`) is set. The parser is
therefore *told* which mode is in force rather than guessing — `Csi::parse_with`
takes a `MarginMode`. Passing that as a `bool` would have put it next to another
`bool` in the same call with nothing to catch them swapped.

## An invalid range is rejected, not clamped

`[[source: vt510-decslrm]]`: "The value of the left margin (Pl) must be less
than the right margin (Pr)", minimum two columns. An inverted or empty range
leaves the margins alone. Clamping would silently give the application a region
it did not ask for.

DECSLRM also homes the cursor, which the spec mandates and which is easy to
forget.

## What the margins bind

The wrap (text resumes at the **left margin**, not column 0), ICH, DCH, and
DECIC/DECDC. The write path measures a run's room to the right margin, which
is the screen edge until DECSLRM sets one, so a run pays one field read and no
per-cell test (`Paint::room`, `baud-core/src/paint.rs`).

`[[source: vt510-decic]]`: "DECIC has no effect outside the scrolling margins",
and the region is the *rectangle* both margin pairs define, not the full height.

## What they do not bind yet

The vertical scrolls. `SU`, `SD`, `IND`, `RI`, `LF`, `IL` and `DL` move whole
rows under DECLRMM, so a scrolling region is a band of rows and never the
rectangle `[[source: vt510-decslrm]]` makes it ("sets the left and right
margins to define the scrolling region"). The row in
[../vt-coverage.md](../vt-coverage.md) states the gap; finishing the margins is
the owner's V10 (`docs/research/notes/v-open-decisions.md`).

Nor do line starts: `CR` goes to column 0 under DECLRMM, not to the left
margin (`baud-core/src/write.rs`); that is step (c) of V10's finish.
