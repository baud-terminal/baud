# W. The cursor trails: where each design came from, what was decided, and what is lost

**Status: a record of provenance, written 2026-10-12 at `b42083b`. Nothing is
built or changed by it.** The ghost was built on 2026-09-20 (`6cecea6`).
The other nine (`quad`, `sweep`, `fire`, `plasma`, `lightning`, `ice`,
`ufo`, `orb`, `liquid`) were researched, specified and built on 2026-09-25 to
2026-09-27. The research's working directory was deleted afterwards. This
note recovers what survives and commits it under `docs/research/notes/w/`,
each file with its SHA-256 (section 1). For each trail it says where the
design came from, what was decided and when, where the measurements are, and
what is lost. Section 8, added 2026-10-25, is the quad's provenance audit
against kitty and the re-derivation that followed it.

**The letter.** W is shared: `w-fallback-fonts.md` is also note W. Cite this
one by its filename. Several letters are already shared this way (A, B, F, G,
J).

**How to read the citations.** A recovered file is cited by its path under
`docs/research/notes/w/`, and section 1 gives the SHA-256 of each as
committed. Dates are the project's local day. A measurement is cited by its
section of MEASUREMENTS.md. A path in the deleted working directory is
written without code formatting, because the tree does not hold it.

## 2. Three earlier claims the recovery corrects

1. **The fire's research is not lost.** The fire report survived whole: the
   prior-art table, the procedural-fire technique, the cost tables, the
   determinism checks, the WCAG luminance counts, and proposals A ("flame
   quad"), B ("ember sparks") and C ("burning fuse", recommended, which is
   what was built). What is lost is the evidence behind it: the source
   files, the outputs and the images (section 1).
2. **The orb had research, and it was not read.** The orb's research wrote
   notes and a Metal harness measured against a CPU reference, and stopped
   on the usage limit before returning a spec. Nothing read those notes
   before the orb was built (`1488b3c`), so the orb was designed in the build,
   in the family's shape (section 5.9).
3. **The fire's design does not come from note V.** It had been cited as
   "proposal C in note V's research". Note V's research has no fire proposal:
   it names no flame, no "burning fuse" and no proposal C. The fire is
   proposal C of the fire report (section 5.4).

## 4. What every effect shares

The contract the effect research was given, from the workflow's own prompt
(2026-09-26), which the effect family built on 2026-09-26 holds:

1. **The cursor's colour only**, premultiplied, with intensity as opacity, so
   one rule serves a dark theme, a light theme and glass.
2. **A pure function** of the pixel, the age since ignition, the ignition's
   seed and the move's geometry. Nothing is kept between frames and the
   shader reads no clock, so a Rust reference can hold the shader to it, and
   the pixel oracle holds both GPU arms to that reference.
3. **The fire's driver**: an ignition on a move of more than two cells, at
   most three a second (`REIGNITE`, 334 ms), and a bounded end.
4. **Bands** one cell row tall over the effect's reach, plus one record a
   particle.
5. **GPU only, at first.** The CPU path drew kitty's quad. It now draws
   every effect by its light (`the_cpu_path_draws_every_effect_by_its_light`).
6. **No texture reads** (the brief excepted the liquid, possibly), so the Rust
   port can match to within a step of 255.
7. **WCAG 2.3.1**, three flashes a second
   ([[source: wcag22-understanding-three-flashes]]). Whether the
   shipped trails meet it is note AL's open question.
8. **Cheap**, priced against the fire.

The prior art behind rules 2 and 3 is kitty's quad and Neovide's VFX, read at
source by both 2026-09-25 studies. kitty's lightning and particle shaders, and
Neovide's VFX at a later commit, were pinned since for note AL
([[source: kitty-trail-lightning-851cb80]],
[[source: kitty-trail-particles-851cb80]],
[[source: neovide-cursor-vfx-c1370a8]]). Reduce Motion is
[[source: apple-nsworkspace-reduce-motion]].

### 5.1 ghost (`6cecea6`, 2026-09-20)

- **Design source:** sfte's `SFTE_CURSOR_TRAIL` ([[source: sfte]]), built to
  baud's blink standard rather than sfte's full wipe. No study:
  this was the trail before the 2026-09-25 decisions, and the quad research
  (section 1) is what found it weak (a one-cell box, linear, 0.1 s, on every
  cell change).
- **Decisions:** on by default, then a style named `ghost` once the
  quad landed. Drawn in the cursor's colour since `84c7726`, under the
  rule of section 4 that every effect is drawn in the cursor's colour.
- **Measured:** MEASUREMENTS.md, "The cursor trails' costs".
- **Lost:** nothing.

### 5.2 quad (`c2e20be`, 2026-09-25)

- **Design source:** kitty's `cursor_trail` at `1e1486e`
  ([[source: kitty-cursor-trail]], [[source: kitty-options-definition]],
  [[source: kitty-trail-slang]]), with Neovide's short-move rule
  ([[source: neovide-cursor-renderer]]) and sfte's 10 ms dwell
  ([[source: sfte]]). The quad report (section 1)
  read kitty, Ghostty, Neovide, Alacritty, WezTerm, iTerm2 and two Ghostty
  shader collections at the commits its pins list names. Its closed form for
  kitty's corners, and its finding that a retargeted quad can be concave,
  are what the build took: hence winding, not kitty's triangle fan.
- **Licence:** kitty is GPL-3.0. The quad's rate choice followed kitty's
  text term for term; its closed form was baud's own. Three routes were
  open: (a) a record that the quad takes only the idea and the constants,
  (b) a clean re-derivation, or (d) leaving it out. **Re-derived on
  2026-10-25 (b)**, because the line-by-line comparison of section 8.2 shows
  the rate choice following kitty's text rather than only its idea.
- **Decisions:** every style is a setting. The quad was the interim
  default until the fire.
- **Measured:** MEASUREMENTS.md, "The cursor trails' costs" (`a_horizontal_move_is_kittys` holds it to kitty's replay).
- **Lost:** the replay outputs and the 66 fetched files. The replay scripts
  are not committed (section 1).

### 5.4 fire (`8211be4`, 2026-09-25; the default until `eb2f75e`)

- **Design source:** proposal C of the fire report (section 1),
  "burning fuse": proposal A's flame, built from fBM value noise on a PCG
  lattice rising off kitty's quad and burning out tail first, plus proposal
  B's closed-form embers. The report names its prior art (Neovide's
  cursor_vfx, smear-cursor.nvim, kitty's blaze and lightning shaders,
  KroneCorylus's and sahaj-b's Ghostty shaders, Atom's activate-power-mode,
  hyperpower, code-blast, VS Code Power Mode, Synesthaxia) and its technique
  sources (iq's fBM, warp, gradient-noise and sfrand articles, Jarzynski and
  Olano's hash paper, the CIE fit of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley, the Metal
  Shading Language specification, metal-benchmarks). It found every GPU
  fire-like cursor effect to be flat amber or a bolt, drawn as a full-screen
  pass. None of the sources is pinned, and all are named, with licences, in
  `docs/research/notes/w/fire-pins.tsv`. The report marks the GPL sources
  (kitty, smear-cursor) as ideas only, and the Shadertoy-derived sparks
  shaders as of unverified licence, and its proposal A says "all new code".
- **Decisions:** the default, over the recommended quad. The cursor's
  colour, not the research's 800-6500 K black-body ramp. That colour also
  met the call for a flame opaque over glass and the light-theme variant the
  research asked for, as one rule. The plasma replaced it as the
  default (2026-09-29, built `eb2f75e`), and it stays by name.
- **Measured:** in the research, GPU +1.5 to 4.5 us a frame on the M5 Max
  at full clock (proposal A's summary; the separate `fire_fragment` pipeline
  measured +1.6 us on a 30-column hop), 5.3 to 33 ms on the CPU (hence GPU
  only), and determinism: zero differing pixels across command buffers and
  pipelines, five pixels one step apart between fast and safe math. In
  baud: the pixel oracle's Rust reference, 31 injected faults its tests
  caught, and the idle-CPU sweep with +0.68% of a core over the quad under
  typing (MEASUREMENTS.md, "The cursor trails' costs"); note AL and the
  flash census for WCAG 2.3.1.
- **Critique:** none of the design. The report labels each claim
  VERIFIED, MEASURED, DERIVED or UNVERIFIED, and the build was held by 31
  injected faults.
- **Lost:** the 80 source files and their SHA-256s, and every output (time1
  to time4, det, lum, cpu_fire) and image. The flame as measured is not
  committed: `fire-flame.metal` is its first write, and the five edits that
  made the measured version are not applied.

### 5.5 plasma (`bc40570`, 2026-09-26; the default since `eb2f75e`)

- **Design source:** `docs/research/notes/w/spec-plasma.json`. Prior art
  named: orbkit's SHDR-13 plasma globe and SHDR-14 sine plasma (MIT per the
  repository's own CREDITS), Future Crew's Second Reality PLZ part
  (Unlicense), Neovide's railgun and torpedo, fielding's electric.glsl (no
  licence, ideas only), nimitz's Plasma Globe through warrenm/Shadertweak
  (Shadertoy content, ideas only), and Lode Vandevenne's plasma tutorial
  (all rights reserved, prose only). No hue cycling, by the contract.
- **Critique:** the only spec with a finished one, verdict "fix". The
  build took its fixes, among them the bright spike its filaments poked ahead
  of the cursor, measured by the critique at 208/255. The critique's licence
  check found nothing lifted from kitty or from Shadertoy.
- **Decisions:** the spec's open questions were built at its proposals and
  are open (section 6).
- **Departure from the spec: the hand.** The spec takes the braid's offset
  across the axis, `w`, and the ions' launch side `perp = (-dir.y, dir.x)`,
  their launch turn and their spin relative to the move's axis. So a
  leftward move is the rightward one turned a half turn, not mirrored: its
  filaments cross the other way and its ions curl to the other side, and a
  cursor moved left and back drew two different trails. Each ignition now
  has a hand, `plasma::Hand`: left for one moving left, right otherwise. A
  left hand negates `w` (`plasma::parts`, `plasma::shade`, `plasma_tube`)
  and the ions' side, turn and spin (`plasma.rs`, `Ion::launch` and
  `Ion::centre`), and snaps an ion's disc box from the mirror image of
  `floor` (`Vfx::instances`). The hand is the ignition's, so a retarget in
  flight cannot flip the braid. Negation is exact: a rightward or vertical
  move keeps every bit, and the `trail` bench's frame hashes are equal on
  all 60 rows. On one seed, a leftward move draws the rightward frame
  mirrored, 0 pixels apart (`baud-render/tests/plasma_hand.rs`), and the
  pixel oracle holds both GPU paths to it (the leftward scene in
  `baud-present-macos/tests/cells.rs`). The reason: a trail must look the
  same whichever way the cursor moves along a line. Up and down moves keep
  the spec's sense. The departure is recorded in the spec too, as its
  `departures` entry.
- **Departure from the spec: every long move lights.** The spec keeps the
  driver's rule, at most three ignitions a second, a long move inside
  `REIGNITE` retargeting the quad and keeping the ignition's age and seed.
  The plasma now lights every long move (`Chase::ignite`), so a move soon
  after the last draws a fresh plasma rather than the burning one at its
  age, whose envelope `(1 - age / 0.55)^2` is 0.67 of a fresh one's 0.1 s
  after it lit, 0.41 at 0.2 s and 0.21 at 0.3 s. Every other effect keeps
  the rule. The reason: a quick move is drawn as bright as a slow one.
- **A repeat is clamped at the cap, not scaled by it.** Above the size
  gate, the flash bound's repeat pass (note AL, O2) scaled every record's
  alpha by the cap, so every pixel of the plasma's pass dimmed by it, to
  95 of 255 over the dark page and 14 over the light. The plasma's
  repeat bands now draw `min(light, cap)` (`effect::Cap`, on the CPU path
  and in `plasma_tube`): every pixel at or under the cap, as before, and
  every pixel already under it whole. Its ions are still dropped on a
  repeat. The other effects still scale, unmeasured clamped.
  `baud-render/tests/flash_bound.rs` and the GPU oracle's repeat scene
  hold it; the census is in MEASUREMENTS.md, "The plasma at full
  strength".
- **Measured:** MEASUREMENTS.md, "The cursor trails' costs"; note AJ section 1.3 and note AL for the flash bound, which

### 5.12 A trail when the cursor hides (2026-11-01)

**When a program hides the cursor while a quad, sweep or effect trail is
drawing, the trail fades out over 0.4 s instead of vanishing.** Until then
the first frame with the cursor hidden dropped the move and put an effect
out. The ghost, which finishes its tenth of a second, is unchanged, and so
is a cursor a program placed before hiding it (helix's).

- **The rule is kitty's** ([[source: kitty-cursor-trail]]): while the
  cursor is hidden the trail's opacity falls by the seconds since the last
  frame over the slow decay, floored at 0 (137-139), and rises at that
  rate, capped at 1, while it is shown (134-136); hidden at 0, nothing is
  drawn (64). baud keeps the same sum in closed form from the last frame
  before each change, on `TAU_SLOW`. Through the fade the corners chase on,
  an effect ages on and the hole stays where the cursor was last drawn; the
  trail is out at the fade's end or its own, whichever comes first.
- **sfte and Neovide end at once.** sfte puts its tail on the cursor and
  draws nothing while its cursor is hidden ([[source: sfte]] 35970-35977),
  which `CSI ? 25 l` sets (34128-34129). Neovide returns before drawing the
  cursor and its effects while the cursor is disabled, and stops animating
  ([[source: neovide-cursor-renderer]] 394-396, 449-451, 461-463).
- **One fade time for every trail**, not each effect's own life: at 60 Hz
  it owes at most 24 frames, where the ufo's own, up to 0.94 s
  (`ufo::LIFE`), would owe up to 57.
- **Shown again**, the trail comes back at the rate it went, as kitty's
  does. The opacity counts from the last frame drawn, so a trail started
  within 0.4 s of a show starts faint and brightens if frames were drawn
  while the cursor was hidden, as they are while output streams, and
  starts whole after a hide that drew nothing for 0.4 s.
- **Reduce Motion:** the default trail is off; a trail named in the config
  fades as under full motion.
- **Every path draws it from one value**, the opacity a smear carries: each
  trail record's alpha is scaled by it and so is the CPU quad's fill.
- **Flashes** (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1, `baud-bench`'s census, CPU frames at
  60 Hz and 14 px, both themes, the app's chase driving the renderer, the
  cursor hidden 50 ms into a 19-column move): every trail passes faded, cut
  off and never hidden. Faded, the most any pixel flashes is no more than
  with the cursor never hidden, in all 18 trail-and-theme rows, and the largest
  flashing area is 6 pt² against 19 pt² never hidden, of a 21,824 pt²
  limit. The control, a quad toggled every 125 ms, counts 4 flashes at a
  pixel. The census program was not kept.
- **Tests:** `a_hidden_cursors_trail_fades_out_over_the_slow_constant`,
  `a_fading_effect_goes_out_at_its_own_end_when_that_is_first`,
  `a_cursor_shown_mid_fade_brings_its_trail_back_at_the_same_rate`,
  `a_fading_trail_is_drawn_part_way_on_the_cpu_path`,
  `a_fade_alone_redraws_the_trails_rows`, and the CPU-against-GPU gate in
  `baud-present-macos/tests/cells.rs`, which draws each smear half faded.

### 5.16 The liquid's back restarts on a second quick jump

**A second long move within `REIGNITE` of the liquid's ignition restarts
the gel's back on that move while the gel still burns, fading or not**, so
it streams its own streak along the second move rather than collapsing onto
the cursor. Every other effect keeps its burning ignition and retargets
the quad, as before. A prototype, behind no setting, in a commit of its
own.

- Until then the second move kept the first ignition. The gel's back is
  taken from kitty's trailing corners along the ignition's direction, and
  its neck, beads and wobble run on the ignition's age, so on a second
  move in another direction the back came in from the side, late in its
  life, and the second jump drew no streak of its own.
- `Chase::restart` lays the ignition again, from where the quad's
  corners were as the move started to the cursor, its seed kept and
  nothing lit, so `REIGNITE`'s count and clock are unchanged. It starts as
  far into the gel's fade-in as the gel was, at most `liquid::APPEAR`: a
  gel not yet fading carries its opacity on, and one within
  `liquid::FADE` of its end is drawn whole again on the frame the move is
  taken on, its opacity rising from wherever the fade had taken it. A gel
  already out is left out, since lighting it again would be an ignition.
- An ignition lit after `REIGNITE` over a gel a restart laid, while that
  gel is not yet fading, starts the same way, so a burst of jumps never
  draws the gel out and back. Over a first gel, which no restart laid --
  a jump long enough that its gel is still whole at `REIGNITE` -- it fades
  in from nothing, as every ignition did before: the burning ignition
  carries how it was laid (`Laid`).
- WCAG 2.3.1, by the `flash` bench, drawn by Metal into an offscreen
  target. At 13 and 32 pt at 2x, 120 Hz, the default ink on the dark and
  the light page, every motion, every case passes before and after, by
  the general and the strict reading, with the cursor's cells and
  without. The restart lights more: the largest flashing area in any
  second grew from 5,091 to 5,816 pt^2, against a threshold of 21,824,
  and the strict reading's from 5,754 to 5,916. Over the bursts at 13 and
  32 pt, at 1x and 2x, 60 and 120 Hz, four inks and four grounds, all 640

## 8. The quad's provenance against kitty, and its re-derivation (2026-10-25)

### 8.3 The route taken

On 8.2's comparison the rate choice as built followed kitty's text term for
term, so a record that the quad takes only kitty's idea and constants (a)
would not have been true of it. It was re-derived (b), from 8.4, rather than
left out (d).

### 8.4 The derivation the rate choice is now written from

At a move's start, each corner of the trail, P_i, chases the same corner of
the cursor's new rectangle, T_i. The rectangle's centre is C.

- **Travel and outward direction.** A corner travels along d_i = T_i - P_i
  and points out of the rectangle along o_i = T_i - C.
- **Lead.** A corner leads the move as far as it travels the way it points:
  lead_i = (d_i . o_i) / (|d_i| |o_i|), the cosine of the angle between
  them. It is 1 for a corner travelling straight out past its own side and
  -1 for one travelling straight in.
- **No direction.** A corner that does not travel (|d_i| < 1e-3 px) has no
  direction, so it takes no part in the next step.
- **Rates.** Over the corners that take part, s_i = (lead_i - lo) / (hi - lo)
  and tau_i = s_i tau_fast + (1 - s_i) tau_slow. The corner leading most
  closes fastest and the one leading least slowest, whichever way the move
  goes. If no two leads differ, no corner leads, and every corner takes
  tau_slow.
- **Motion.** Each corner closes its gap exponentially and halves it every
  tau / 10. tau is the time to close all but 2^-10 of the gap, the unit
  kitty's cursor_trail_decay option is given in. So P_i(t) = T_i + (P_i - T_i)
  2^(-10 t / tau_i). A move is over when every gap is under half a pixel on
  both axes, at tau / 10 log2(gap / 0.5).

Neovide's cursor renderer publishes the same idea under MIT
([[source: neovide-cursor-renderer]] 191-208 and 504-522): the dot product
of a corner's normalised travel and its normalised direction, spread over
the four corners, interpolating two animation lengths. It is cited as a
published statement of the idea, and none of its lines is ported. Three
details differ from baud's:

- Its outward direction is the corner's relative position normalised in
  cell units, so it does not change with the cell's aspect ratio. baud's is
  the pixel offset T_i - C.
- It measures travel from the corner's previous destination, not from where
  the corner is.
- When the four alignments have no spread, or are NaN, it gives each corner
  alignment 1.0, the short, leading length. baud gives every corner tau_slow.

Every corner of a rectangle is half a diagonal from C, so |o_i| is one
number, and the lead equals kitty's dot up to rounding. That is why a move
from rest draws as it did.

**The one case where it differs from kitty's code** is a corner already at
its target. kitty gives such a corner 0 and keeps it in the spread; here it
takes no part (`a_corner_already_home_takes_no_part_in_the_spread`). A long
move from rest moves every corner, so the case can only arise from a
retarget whose corner lands within 1e-3 px of its new target. It is visible:
in that test's retarget the old code gave [0.4, 0.1, 0.117, 0.259] and this
gives [0.4, 0.1, 0.132, 0.4], so the top-left corner, leading least, takes
0.4 s where it took 0.259 s, and the bottom-left a little longer too. It is
on section 6's list.

### 8.5 What is still kitty's, and the limits of this

kitty contributed the idea of four corners chasing at their own rates, the
defaults (0.1 s, 0.4 s, two cells), the half-pixel end, the rest before a
trail follows, and the corner order. These are ideas, rules and numbers, and
none of them is code. `a_horizontal_move_is_kittys` checks baud against
numbers printed by a replay of kitty's code, and those numbers stay. The
re-derivation was written from 8.4 by an author who had read
`cursor_trail.c` for the audit. It is not a clean room in the two-team
sense. None of this is legal advice.
