# Lightning trail -- design notes (research only, nothing in the repo changed)

Scratch dir: this file's directory. Sources and their sha256 are in `pins.txt`.
`model/model.rs` is a standalone f32 model of this design, built with plain
`rustc -O` outside the repo. Running it (`model/results.txt`, sha256 `fe66c6a0...`) printed:

```
vertices: endpoints nonzero 0; max |y| 0.989 cells; fork max |offset| 1.183 cells; min spacing 0.375 cells; max slope 1.19 (50.0 deg); mean |angle| 17.5 deg
pixels lit at peak (sampled): 112953; reach needed 0.963 cells (REACH 2.2); window vs all: 239 pixels differ, worst 0.0000 of 255; worst peaks a pixel 1
area unit 34 px (13-14 pt at 2x): worst 341x256 CSS window has 2174 CSS px at alpha>=0.1 = 2.5% of the field (threshold 25%)
area unit 172 px (72 pt at 2x): worst 341x256 CSS window has 12727 CSS px at alpha>=0.1 = 14.6% of the field (threshold 25%)
```

The model covers 8 moves x 399 seeds for the vertex bounds. It covers 5 moves x 3 seeds
at 169 ages (1/480 s apart) for the pixel checks. `model/img/*.png` show the look at ages
0.02, 0.045, 0.09, 0.16 and 0.24 s, on a dark and a light theme. `sheet-*.png` stacks the five.

## The contract as found

The contract is read from the working tree, not HEAD. `effect.rs` and the `EffectU` refactor are uncommitted.

- `EffectU` is a 24-float prefix, then `k[8]`, for 32 floats and 128 bytes, bound with
  `setFragmentBytes` at `[[buffer(0)]]` (`metal.rs` 1306).
- The prefix is: `c[4]` (0-7), `hole` (8-11), `origin` (12-13), `tail` (14-15),
  `dir` (16-17), `len` 18, `unit` 19, `age` 20, `life` 21, `seed` 22 (a `uint`), and `reach` 23.
- The records are `Kind::Band = 4` and `Kind::Particle = 5`. `glyph_vertex` gives a
  particle a uv from -1 to 1. Lightning adds **no new Kind**. It needs one late
  pipeline, `lightning_fragment`, and one `Effect::Lightning` arm in every match.
- Shared shader helpers are `fx_hash`, `fx_unit`, `fx_lattice`, `fx_distance` and
  `fx_in_hole`. Lightning reuses all five and adds `fx_segment`, `bolt_offset`,
  `bolt_vertex` and `bolt_fork`.

## Look

A long move (more than 2 cells) makes a thin, jagged bolt from where the cursor was to
where it is. Over 40 ms it draws itself from the old cell to the new one, like a
stepped leader. It holds full for 18 ms. Then it fades over 0.25 s. The core thins as
it fades, and a soft glow lingers slightly longer.

Other elements:
- Up to three short forks leave the main channel at 25-45 degrees.
- A soft corona rings the cursor when the bolt lands.
- Ten small sparks splash out of the cursor at the landing and fall.

Everything is the cursor's colour. Intensity is opacity. The cursor's own rectangle
stays bare. There is no flicker, no re-strike and no screen flash. The bolt's shape
never changes during its life. This is kitty's jagged-line idea done
with a real midpoint-displacement channel (Hoffman, Reed & Wyvill). It has a leader
that travels, a single strike, and landing sparks. It is not fire recoloured: there
is no noise field and no rising body.

## Geometry (all closed form)

- **The path.** Take `tail`, `dir` and `len` from `Ignition`. Let `n = (-dir.y, dir.x)`
  and `c = unit` (the cell height in px).
- **Levels.** Start with `L = 0`. While `L < 8 && len >= 0.30*c*2^(L+1)`, add one to
  `L`. Then `M = 2^L` segments, each at least 0.30 cells long. Every comparison is
  exact, so Rust and MSL agree.
- **The offset at u in [0,1]** is midpoint displacement written as its Schauder
  (Levy-Ciesielski) sum. Only one tent per level is nonzero at any u:

      y(u) = sum_{l<L} min(0.8*len/2^(l+1), 0.25*c) * (2*fx_lattice(int2(floor(u*2^l), l), seed ^ 0x51ED270B) - 1) * tent_l(u),
      tent_l(u) = 1 - |2*frac(u*2^l) - 1|

  At a dyadic u this equals the recursive midpoint displacement exactly. `y(0)` and
  `y(1)` are exactly 0, so the ends are pinned.
- **Vertex j** is `tail + dir*(len*j/M) + n*y(j/M)`, a piecewise-linear bolt.
- **The distance from p to the main bolt.** Let `u = dot(p-tail,dir)/len`,
  `k = clamp(floor(u*M),0,M-1)` and `a0 = max(k-1,0)`. Take four vertices
  `w_i = vertex(min(a0+i, M))` for i = 0..3. The distance is the minimum over the
  three segments `(w0,w1)`, `(w1,w2)` and `(w2,w3)`.
- **Why the three-segment window is exact.** Projection onto `dir` is 1-Lipschitz. So
  the nearest bolt point to p lies within one segment (at least 0.30 c) of `u`
  whenever `dist <= 0.30 c`. Past that distance the glow is below 0.5/255, because the
  glow cutoff is `2.36*0.12 = 0.28 c`. Measured: 239 pixels differ from the minimum
  over every segment, by 0.0000 of 255.
- **Bounds.** Proven: `|y| <= 0.25 c * L <= 2.0 c`. Measured maximum: 0.99 c. The mean
  kink angle is 17.5 degrees (Reed & Wyvill are quoted secondhand as about 16) and the
  maximum is 50 degrees.
- **Forks.** For k in 0..3:
  - `h0 = fx_hash(k + fx_hash(seed ^ 0xA24BAED4))`. The fork exists if
    `unit(h0) < 0.75`, and only when `len >= 5 c`. Take `h1..h4 = fx_hash` chained.
  - The root is at `q = 0.25(k+1)` and `y_r = y(q)`. Only levels 0 and 1 are nonzero at
    a quarter point, so `bolt_offset(q, min(L,2))` is exact.
  - Side: if `|y_r| > 0.6c`, `side = -sign(y_r)`, so it points back toward the axis.
    Otherwise the side is `±1` from `unit(h1)`.
  - Direction: `fd = normalize(dir + n*side*(0.466 + 0.534*unit(h2)))`, which is
    25-45 degrees.
  - Length: `lf = min((0.18+0.14 unit(h3))*len, 1.2c)`.
  - Tip: `root + fd*lf`. Elbow: `root + fd*0.5lf + perp(fd)*(unit(h4)-0.5)*0.3lf`.
  - Two segments each. Measured maximum offset: 1.18 c. Proven: at most 2.0 c.
- **Emitter.** This is a deviation from the fire pattern. See Risks.
  - The emitter is the **fixed axis box**
    `[tail-n*hw, head-n*hw, head+n*hw, tail+n*hw]`, with
    `hw = 0.5(|n.x| cw + |n.y| ch)`.
  - The hole is the head rect at ignition.
  - Both are constant for the whole ignition, so the reach mask is time-invariant.
- **Reach.** `REACH = 2.2` cells. From the proof: `2.0 + 0.28 (glow cut) - 0.2`
  (the least box half-width, at a cell aspect of 0.40) `= 2.08`, which is covered by
  2.2. Measured need: 0.96 cells.

## Timing

All values are seconds; `k[3..5]` live in `EffectU.k`.

- `T_LEAD = 0.040`: the leader crosses the path. A pixel's arrival is
  `tau = T_LEAD * clamp(u,0,1)`.
- `T_RISE = 0.012`: `rise = smoothstep(tau, tau+T_RISE, age)`.
- `T_FALL = 0.070`: `fall = 1 - smoothstep(T_FALL, LIFE, age)`.
- `LIFE = 0.32` is below `REIGNITE = 0.334`, so at most one bolt is alive at a time.
  That is a `const` assertion.
- End: `end() = max(LIFE, T_LEAD + 0.20) = LIFE`, since sparks die by 0.24 s.

**Invariant.** `T_LEAD + T_RISE <= T_FALL`. Every pixel finishes rising before any
pixel starts falling. With the geometry fixed, a pixel's opacity only rises and then
only falls: it has one peak.

## Opacity per pixel

`a = color.a * saturate(core*fall^2 + (glow + corona)*fall) * rise`, where:

- `w = max(0.06c, 1px) * (0.45 + 0.55 fall)`. The core thins monotonically.
- `core = max(exp(-(dm/w)^4), 0.7 exp(-(df/(0.7w))^4))`. This is Reed & Wyvill's
  sharpened channel with n = 4, taken from the RPI report.
- `glow = 0.40 * max(exp(-(dm/(0.12c))^2), 0.6 exp(-(df/(0.09c))^2))`.
- `corona = 0.30 * exp(-(dh/(0.20c))^2) * smoothstep(T_LEAD, T_LEAD+T_RISE, age)`,
  where `dh` is the distance to the hole rect, measured outside it.
- Cores and glows combine by `max`, not a sum. Hoffman's max blend gives no bright
  joints.

The eight numbers in `k[8]` are `[0.06, 0.12, 0.40, 0.040, 0.012, 0.070, 0.30, 0.20]`:
core width, glow width, glow peak, T_LEAD, T_RISE, T_FALL, corona peak and corona
width.

The bolt's shape constants are `#define`s in the shader, pinned against the Rust
consts by a `metallib.rs` test in the style of `SHAPE_SLOTS`:
- JAG 0.8, CAP 0.25, S_MIN 0.30, LEVELS 8;
- the salts;
- FORK_MIN 5.0 and FORK_P 0.75;
- tan 0.466..1.0, length 0.18/0.14/1.2, elbow 0.3, inward threshold 0.6.

## Particles: sparks (Rust, closed form, Kind::Particle)

There are 10 sparks.
- **Hash:** `h = Hash(seed ^ (i*0x9E3779B9) ^ 0x3C6EF372).next()`, chained to h1..h4.
- **Birth and life:** born at `T_LEAD`, when the bolt lands. `a = age - T_LEAD`.
  `life = 0.10 + 0.10 h3`.
- **Angle:** `theta = atan2(dir) + (h1-0.5)*1.6*pi`. That is a splash of ±144°,
  never straight back along the bolt.
- **Speed:** `(5 + 5 h2) c/s`. **Drag:** `K = 14/s`. **Gravity:** `8 c/s²`
  downward, so sparks fall where embers rise.
- **Start:** `head + (cos,sin)(theta)*0.55c`, just outside the cursor.
- **Position:** `start + v(1-e^{-Ka})/K + (0, 0.5 g a²)`.
- **Radius:** `c(0.035 + 0.025 h4)`. **Glow:** `(1 - a/life)²`, so a spark is born at
  full brightness and only fades.
- **Travel:** at most 1.4 c from the head.
- **Drawing:** the existing soft disc. Share `fire_ember` as `fx_particle`.

## MSL sketch

```metal
// ---- The lightning, `baud-render/src/lightning.rs`. k[0] core half-width,
// k[1] glow width, k[2] glow peak, k[3] leader crossing, k[4] rise, k[5] fall
// start, k[6] corona peak, k[7] corona width -- cells or seconds.
#define BOLT_JAG 0.8
#define BOLT_CAP 0.25
#define BOLT_S_MIN 0.30
#define BOLT_LEVELS 8u
#define BOLT_SALT 0x51ED270Bu
#define FORK_SALT 0xA24BAED4u
#define FORK_MIN 5.0
#define FORK_P 0.75

static inline float fx_segment(float2 p, float2 a, float2 b) {
    float2 ba = b - a;
    float2 pa = p - a;
    float h = clamp(dot(pa, ba) / max(dot(ba, ba), 1e-6), 0.0, 1.0);
    return length(pa - ba * h);
}

static inline uint bolt_levels(float len, float c) {
    uint l = 0u;
    while (l < BOLT_LEVELS && len >= BOLT_S_MIN * c * float(1u << (l + 1u))) l++;
    return l;
}

// Midpoint displacement at u as its Schauder sum: one tent a level.
static inline float bolt_offset(float u, uint lv, constant EffectU& f) {
    float y = 0.0;
    for (uint l = 0u; l < lv; l++) {
        float n = float(1u << l);
        float x = u * n;
        float i = floor(x);
        float t = x - i;
        float tent = 1.0 - abs(2.0 * t - 1.0);
        float xi = 2.0 * fx_lattice(int2(int(i), int(l)), f.seed ^ BOLT_SALT) - 1.0;
        float d = min(BOLT_JAG * f.len / (2.0 * n), BOLT_CAP * f.unit);
        y += d * xi * tent;
    }
    return y;
}

static inline float2 bolt_vertex(int j, int m, uint lv, float2 n, constant EffectU& f) {
    float u = float(j) / float(m);
    return f.tail + f.dir * (f.len * u) + n * bolt_offset(u, lv, f);
}

static inline float bolt_fork(float2 p, int k, uint lv, float2 n, constant EffectU& f) {
    uint h0 = fx_hash(uint(k) + fx_hash(f.seed ^ FORK_SALT));
    if (fx_unit(h0) >= FORK_P) return INFINITY;
    uint h1 = fx_hash(h0); uint h2 = fx_hash(h1); uint h3 = fx_hash(h2); uint h4 = fx_hash(h3);
    float q = 0.25 * float(k + 1);
    float yr = bolt_offset(q, min(lv, 2u), f);
    float side = abs(yr) > 0.6 * f.unit ? -sign(yr) : (fx_unit(h1) < 0.5 ? 1.0 : -1.0);
    float2 fd = normalize(f.dir + n * (side * (0.466 + 0.534 * fx_unit(h2))));
    float lf = min((0.18 + 0.14 * fx_unit(h3)) * f.len, 1.2 * f.unit);
    float2 r = f.tail + f.dir * (f.len * q) + n * yr;
    float2 e = r + fd * (0.5 * lf) + float2(-fd.y, fd.x) * ((fx_unit(h4) - 0.5) * 0.3 * lf);
    float2 t = r + fd * lf;
    return min(fx_segment(p, r, e), fx_segment(p, e, t));
}

static inline float4 bolt_light(float2 p, float4 color, constant EffectU& f) {
    if (fx_in_hole(p, f)) return float4(0.0);
    if (f.age >= f.life) return float4(0.0);
    float c = f.unit;
    if (fx_distance(p, f) / c > f.reach) return float4(0.0);
    float u = dot(p - f.tail, f.dir) / f.len;
    float tau = f.k[3] * clamp(u, 0.0, 1.0);
    float rise = smoothstep(tau, tau + f.k[4], f.age);
    if (rise <= 0.0) return float4(0.0);
    float fall = 1.0 - smoothstep(f.k[5], f.life, f.age);
    float2 n = float2(-f.dir.y, f.dir.x);
    uint lv = bolt_levels(f.len, c);
    int m = int(1u << lv);
    int a0 = max(clamp(int(floor(u * float(m))), 0, m - 1) - 1, 0);
    float2 w0 = bolt_vertex(a0, m, lv, n, f);
    float2 w1 = bolt_vertex(min(a0 + 1, m), m, lv, n, f);
    float2 w2 = bolt_vertex(min(a0 + 2, m), m, lv, n, f);
    float2 w3 = bolt_vertex(min(a0 + 3, m), m, lv, n, f);
    float dm = min(min(fx_segment(p, w0, w1), fx_segment(p, w1, w2)), fx_segment(p, w2, w3));
    float df = INFINITY;
    if (f.len >= FORK_MIN * c) {
        for (int k = 0; k < 3; k++) df = min(df, bolt_fork(p, k, lv, n, f));
    }
    float2 hc = (f.hole.xy + f.hole.zw) * 0.5;
    float2 hh = (f.hole.zw - f.hole.xy) * 0.5;
    float dh = length(max(abs(p - hc) - hh, 0.0));
    float w = max(f.k[0] * c, 1.0) * (0.45 + 0.55 * fall);
    float gw = f.k[1] * c;
    float km = (dm / w) * (dm / w);
    float kf = (df / (0.7 * w)) * (df / (0.7 * w));
    float core = max(exp(-km * km), 0.7 * exp(-kf * kf));
    float gm = dm / gw;
    float gf = df / (0.75 * gw);
    float glow = f.k[2] * max(exp(-gm * gm), 0.6 * exp(-gf * gf));
    float gc = dh / (f.k[7] * c);
    float corona = f.k[6] * exp(-gc * gc) * smoothstep(f.k[3], f.k[3] + f.k[4], f.age);
    float a = color.a * saturate(core * fall * fall + (glow + corona) * fall) * rise;
    return float4(color.rgb * a, a);
}

fragment float4 lightning_fragment(GOut in [[stage_in]], constant EffectU& f [[buffer(0)]]) {
    if (in.kind == KIND_PARTICLE) return fx_particle(in);  // the ember disc, shared
    return bolt_light(in.pos.xy - f.origin, in.color, f);
}
```

**Cost per band pixel**, compared with the fire:
- Hashes: 4 vertices x up to 8 levels = 32 PCG hashes for the main bolt. The forks add
  up to 3 x (1 shared + 5 + 2 levels x 1). A cheap first `fx_hash(seed^FORK_SALT)` can
  be hoisted. Total: at most ~52 hashes.
- Distances: 3 main segments, up to 6 fork segments, 1 quad distance and 1 box.
- `exp`: 5.
- No texture.
- The fire runs 32 hashes (4 octaves x 4 lattice x 2), 2 quad distances and noise mixes.
- So lightning costs about 1.0-1.5x the fire per pixel. It has 10% more band area
  (reach 2.2 against 2.0) and 0.32 s of life against 0.45-0.85 s.
- The cost that dominates in this repo is the frame count at ~0.24 ms of Metal
  submission each. Lightning's shorter life means fewer frames per ignition: about 39
  at 120 Hz, against the fire's 54-102.

## Photosensitivity (WCAG 2.3.1, local copy w3c/understanding-2.3.1.txt)

A general flash is a pair of opposing relative-luminance changes of at least 10%, with
the darker state below 0.80. There are two ways to pass: no more than three flashes
per second, or a combined concurrent flashing area of no more than 25% of a
341x256 CSS-px field. This design passes both, independently:

1. **Rate.** A pixel's opacity has exactly one peak per ignition. That holds by
   construction and was measured: the worst count was 1 peak per pixel, at 1/255
   hysteresis. Ignitions are at least 334 ms apart. `LIFE` (0.32 s) is below that, so
   ignitions never overlap. That gives at most 3 flashes per second at any pixel, the
   red-flash count included when the cursor is red. Sparks are born at full brightness
   and only fade, and a moving disc makes one rise and one fall per pixel it crosses.
2. **Area.** At the peak frame, alpha is at least 0.1 on 2.5% of the worst 341x256
   window at a 13-14 pt font (unit 34 device px, 2x). At 72 pt (unit 172) it is 14.6%.
   Both are under 25%.

The rise is 12 ms, so a transition takes 1-2 frames. The fall takes 250 ms, a slope of
at most 0.004 alpha per ms.

**Prior art fails exactly here.** kitty, and 0xhckr before it, multiply the whole bolt by
`0.82 + 0.18 sin(95*age)` with age normalised over 0.14 s. That is 108 Hz, which
aliases to about 12 Hz on a 120 Hz panel, at ±18% amplitude inside one 140 ms strike.
The bolt is re-seeded on every cursor move, with no rate limit. fielding's `electric.glsl`
has four problems:
- it re-shapes the arc every frame (fbm in time), a continuous crackle;
- it pulses the thickness with `sin(40t)` at 6.4 Hz, ±50%;
- it adds a whole-screen `sin(25t)` wash at 4 Hz;
- it shakes the whole screen at 14-17 Hz.
godotshaders' CC0 lightning scales brightness by `hash12(TIME)`, a random strobe every
frame. The design here has no oscillating term at all.

## What a Rust reference must port

For the pixel oracle, port `bolt_light` exactly, in its operation order:
- `fx_segment`;
- `bolt_levels` (integer comparisons);
- `bolt_offset`, keeping the per-level order `n`, `x`, `floor`, `t`, `tent`,
  `xi` via `fx_lattice(i, level)`, `d = min(...)`, and `y += d*xi*tent`;
- the four clamped vertices;
- `bolt_fork`, where `normalize` becomes `v / sqrt(dot)` and `sign`;
- the box `dh`, the `w`/`core`/`glow`/`corona` exps, `saturate` and premultiply.

`fx_distance`, `fx_lattice`, `fx_hash` and `fx_unit` are already ported as the fire's
reference. The disc is already ported as the ember.

Tolerance: one step of 255 and a handful of pixels, as for the fire. Metal
fast-math may fuse `y += d*xi*tent` into an fma and approximate `normalize` and `exp`,
at about an ulp each.

**The oracle is blind to** the sparks (CPU) and to anything the shader and the
reference get wrong the same way. Unit tests have to own those:
- endpoints pinned, `y(0) == y(1) == 0`;
- `|y| <= CAP*L*c`;
- spacing >= S_MIN c;
- slope and kink statistics;
- the same seed gives the same shape and a different seed a different one;
- the three-segment window equals brute force for pixels within S_MIN;
- a unimodality test: each band pixel sampled at 480 Hz shows at most 1 peak;
- band coverage at REACH 2.2, with a cell aspect of 0.40;
- spark birth, travel and glow;
- `LIFE < REIGNITE`;
- the shader defines pinned against the Rust consts.

## Risks

- **The emitter deviates from the fire pattern.** `Vfx.emitter` for lightning must be
  the fixed axis box, not kitty's chasing quad.
  - With the chasing quad, the bolt's tail is cut by the reach within about 40 ms: kitty's
    trailing corners halve their gap every 40 ms.
  - A time-varying reach mask also breaks the single-peak proof.
  - This needs a small `effect.rs` or driver hook, for example
    `Effect::emitter(ignition, chase)`.
  - The CPU path draws kitty's quad, and the GPU draws a bolt over a box the CPU never
    shows. The two paths diverge more than they do for the fire.
- **The hole is fixed to the head rect at ignition.** The fire's hole follows the
  cursor. On a retarget within 334 ms, the bolt stays on its old path, and the new
  cursor cell is not masked for up to 0.32 s.
- **REACH comes from the loose bound** (2.2 cells), while the measured need is about
  1.0. Bands are about twice as tall as strictly needed. A Takagi-type bound (the sum
  of tents averages 2/3) would allow about 1.6, but that is not proven here.
- **Per-pixel hashing.** About 52 hashes worst case, versus the fire's 32. The cost is
  small against the frame-submission cost.
- **Unread at source.** Reed & Wyvill 1994 returned 403 from the ACM, so their constants
  (for example the ~16 degree mean angle) are secondhand, via the RPI report. Shadertoy
  was not fetched (the API needs a key, and its default licence is CC BY-NC-SA).
- **Large fonts.** At 72 pt the flashing area reaches 14.6% of the field. It is still
  under 25%, and the rate criterion holds regardless.
- **Readability.** The bolt crosses text for 0.32 s.

## Owner questions (taste only)

1. **Strike or arc.** The strike (recommended) has a fixed shape and one flash per
   ignition. The "electricity" arc re-shapes continuously: its offsets are interpolated
   between two seeds over the life. Each pixel under the moving line would see several
   small flashes, though the area stays tiny.
2. Draw kitty's quad faintly under the bolt on the GPU, or the bolt alone
   (recommended)?
3. A long move within 334 ms of the last ignition: keep the old bolt (proposed), or
   let the head follow, which is a re-shape?
4. Sparks and corona at the landing: on (proposed) or off?
