FONTLOG for baud's embedded font: baud-app/fonts/built/

This is the SIL Open Font License's FONTLOG for the modified fonts in this directory:
what they are, what changed against what they came from, what was chosen in the
meantime, and what is still wrong. The licence is OFL.txt and the authors are listed in
AUTHORS.txt. MANIFEST records how the files are built and pins every input and output.

On macOS, scripts/bundle-macos.sh puts this directory's OFL.txt, FONTLOG.txt,
AUTHORS.txt and MANIFEST, glyphs/languages/hebrew/'s OFL.txt and SOURCE, and
glyphs/languages/devanagari/SOURCE in the app bundle beside the other embedded fonts'
licences; the nix package (flake.nix) carries the same files. Any other Linux package
must carry them too.


THE FILES

Regular.ttf     JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular plus Hebrew and Noto Sans Devanagari. The
                variant is A-L2.
Bold.ttf        JetBrains Mono 2.304 Bold plus Hebrew and Noto Sans Devanagari Bold. The
                variant is A-L2.
Italic.ttf      JetBrains Mono 2.304 Italic plus Hebrew, leaning right, and Noto Sans
                Devanagari, upright. The variant is A-L2.
BoldItalic.ttf  JetBrains Mono 2.304 Bold Italic plus Hebrew, leaning right, and Noto Sans
                Devanagari Bold, upright. The variant is A-L2.

All four are built by scripts/build-fonts.sh from baud-app/fonts/jetbrains-mono/,
baud-app/fonts/glyphs/languages/hebrew/ (see SOURCE there) and Noto Sans Devanagari
2.007's variable font, fetched by the URL and sha256 in
baud-app/fonts/glyphs/languages/devanagari/SOURCE. It builds them in two stages, the
Hebrew and then the Devanagari (THE DEVANAGARI), and refuses to write bytes other than
these sha256s:

The Hebrew stage's Regular and Bold are the gated final3 files (note AC 4b; ea07610d... and c508ee5a...) with the two
conformance fixes in the CHANGELOG and, since 2026-10-24, one redrawn letter, dalet,
since 2026-10-25 Bold's zayin with square head ends (CHANGELOG), since 2026-10-25
the glyphs baud draws itself (BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS), since 2026-10-26 meteg kept in
its cell (CHANGELOG), and since 2026-10-28 zayin's head narrowed from the left
(CHANGELOG), and nothing else.
Before the dalet: take the 6-byte hebr script record out of GSUB and of GPOS and both
tables were final3's byte for byte, and every other table but name (nameID 7) and head
(its checksum) was final3's. The dalet changes that glyph's outline, its anchors in
GPOS and head's checksum alone; the zayin changes Bold's and Bold Italic's zayin
outline (and Bold's loca, for its length), Bold Italic's left side bearing for it,
and head's checksum alone. The gates were run again on these bytes on 2026-10-08,
2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25 (CHANGELOG).

Running scripts/build-fonts.sh --check rebuilds all four and compares them with MANIFEST.
scripts/build-fonts.sh --italics rebuilds the two italics with the rounds' correction
off (both its parts) and requires the plain shear's earlier gated bytes (note AC 4b),
so the correction is the only thing the italics add to the shear.


WHAT CHANGED AGAINST STOCK JETBRAINS MONO 2.304
(each file's Hebrew stage against the stock file of its own style, measured with
fontTools 4.63.0; THE DEVANAGARI says what the merge adds to it and changes in it)

- 135 code points added, 88 of them in U+0591-U+05F4 -- every character the
  Unicode Character Database 17.0 assigns in the Hebrew block:
  - the 31 cantillation marks, U+0591-U+05AF (BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS);
  - the 27 letters, U+05D0-U+05EA, and U+05EF YOD TRIANGLE and the Yiddish
    ligatures U+05F0-U+05F2 (BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS);
  - the points: the vowels (nikud), dagesh, meteg, rafe, the shin and sin dots, the
    upper and lower dots and qamats qatan (U+05B0-U+05BD, U+05BF, U+05C1, U+05C2,
    U+05C4, U+05C5, U+05C7);
  - maqaf, paseq, sof pasuq, nun hafukha, geresh and gershayim;
  - U+20AA NEW SHEQEL SIGN, baud's own drawing (BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS);
  - and the 46 Hebrew presentation forms in U+FB1D-U+FB4F (BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS).
  The glyph count goes from 1743 to 1878, and the stock glyph order is an exact
  prefix, so every existing glyph keeps its ID.
- No stock glyph changed: 0 glyf records, 0 advances and 0 cmap entries changed or
  lost. GSUB is byte-identical but for one added script record, hebr (below): with it
  taken out, every byte is stock's. The 20 combining points, the 31 cantillation
  marks and varika advance 0, and the 31 letters, 6 punctuation marks, the sheqel
  sign and the 45 other presentation forms advance 600 (one cell).
- GPOS goes from 5 lookups to 35. The 5 stock lookups are unchanged. The new ones are
  mark-to-base (type 4), for the points and cantillation marks on the letters, and
  four mark-to-mark (type 6): meteg on the vowel before it; a cantillation mark below
  left of the point or meteg before it; yetiv and dehi right of the point before
  them; and a postpositive mark above left of a holam. They are appended to the
  existing mark and mkmk features. In mkmk, after meteg's mark-to-mark, a chained
  context (type 8) keeps meteg in its cell: in a cluster that carries a meteg it
  places the vowel and meteg again, left of the vowel when meteg follows it and
  right of it when meteg comes before it through CGJ, through 24 mark-to-base
  lookups that no feature names (build/meteg.py). GDEF classes the 135 new glyphs (83 base, 52 mark;
  every stock glyph keeps its class) and gains four mark filtering sets, one for each
  new mark-to-mark lookup.
- GSUB and GPOS each gain a hebr script record, between DFLT and latn, naming DFLT's
  Script table: the same default language system and feature indices (in GPOS, mark
  and mkmk). Microsoft's 'Developing OpenType Fonts for Hebrew Script' registers hebr,
  and HarfBuzz's Hebrew shaper applies GPOS only when the script it chose is hebr.
  baud selects latn first, so what baud draws does not change.
- OS/2 sets the Hebrew Unicode range bit (ulUnicodeRange1 bit 11), the Alphabetic
  Presentation Forms range bit (bit 62, ulUnicodeRange2 bit 30) and the Hebrew code
  page bit (ulCodePageRange1 bit 5).
- In the name table, the family is "Proto Hebrew Mono C", and the same in Italic.ttf
  and BoldItalic.ttf (below). nameID 3 carries the
  prototype tag. nameID 0 carries JetBrains' copyright line followed by Microsoft's,
  with the Reserved Font Name Cascadia Code. nameID 7 carries both trademark notices,
  each as its source font has it: "JetBrains Mono is a trademark of JetBrains s.r.o.
  Cascadia Code is a trademark of the Microsoft group of companies." (OFL-FAQ
  1.1-update7, Q3.7: "Any trademark notices must remain in any derivative fonts").
  nameIDs 8, 9, 11 and 12 are still JetBrains' manufacturer, designers and URLs, and
  nameID 13 is still JetBrains Mono's OFL sentence. baud draws nothing from the name
  table; since 2026-10-20 it reads the family to answer a program that asks which
  font is in use (xterm's OSC 50), and the answer is this family. The family name
  is an open call; see below.


THE DEVANAGARI

The Devanagari is Noto Sans Devanagari 2.007 (Copyright 2022 The Noto Project
Authors; OFL 1.1 with no Reserved Font Name), taken from its variable font, wght
100-900 and wdth 62.5-100, which glyphs/languages/devanagari/SOURCE pins. Regular and
Italic take it instanced at wght 400, Bold and Bold Italic at wght 700, its width
axis kept (fontTools' instancer, hebrew/build/instance.py). These are third-party
glyphs for the interim; baud's own Devanagari, designed for the grid, is to replace
them. glyphs/languages/devanagari/build/merge.py merges each instance into its
style's Hebrew stage (measured with fontTools 4.63.0, against that stage):

- 783 glyphs appended after every existing glyph, so no glyph ID moves: every Noto
  glyph a code point the face did not map reaches, every glyph a Noto lookup names,
  and their components, in Noto's order, at Noto's units (both fonts are 1000 to the
  em, and no scale is applied), with Noto's advances. The glyph count goes from 1878
  to 2661 (the italics from 1865 to 2648).
- 225 code points mapped, in every cmap subtable that can hold them: the Devanagari
  block U+0900-U+097F, Vedic Extensions U+1CD0-U+1CF9 but U+1CF7, Common Indic Number
  Forms U+A830-U+A839, Devanagari Extended U+A8E0-U+A8FF, U+11B00-U+11B09 of
  Devanagari Extended-A, ZWNJ, ZWJ, U+20B9 INDIAN RUPEE SIGN and U+20F0 COMBINING
  ASTERISK ABOVE. Noto's glyphs for code points the face already maps (the ASCII
  punctuation and digits, the space, the dotted circle) are not mapped: the face
  keeps its own.
- GSUB gains Noto's dev2 and deva scripts, with their language systems (MAR, NEP,
  SAN, SAT), Noto's 171 lookups and its features for them, and one single
  substitution more under nukt: the face's dotted circle to Noto's. The shapers put
  U+25CC before a mark with no base after locl and ccmp, from the face's cmap, which
  gives JetBrains Mono's circle; Noto's mark positioning is made for its own, so in
  a Devanagari run the circle is Noto's and the mark sits where Noto put it. U+25CC
  outside a Devanagari run is JetBrains Mono's. GSUB goes from 460 lookups to 632
  (the italics from 454 to 626). GPOS gains the same scripts and Noto's 28 lookups,
  from 35 to 63; Noto's one lookup that names its space, a chained context of dist,
  names the face's space instead.
- GDEF classes the new glyphs as Noto does, appends Noto's six mark filtering sets
  after the face's seven (the lookups that use them are renumbered to match), and
  carries Noto's variation store for GPOS's anchor deltas, which makes it version
  1.3.
- The width axis: fvar with the one axis wdth, 62.5 to 100, default 100, flagged
  HIDDEN_AXIS ("The axis should not be exposed directly in user interfaces"; it is
  there for a program narrowing a Devanagari word to fit it, as the registered
  axis's text suggests: "Applications may choose to select a width variant in a
  variable font automatically in order to fit a span of text into a target width"),
  and one named instance, the default, under the face's own subfamily; Noto's avar;
  gvar and HVAR with Noto's outline and advance variations for the Devanagari and
  none for any other glyph; and the STAT table OpenType requires of a variable font,
  with the family's weight, width and slope. At every width JetBrains Mono's and the
  Hebrew's glyphs keep their outlines and advances.
- The values the new glyphs change: OS/2 sets the Devanagari range bit
  (ulUnicodeRange1 bit 15), its xAvgCharWidth is the average of every non-zero
  advance (600 -> 581 in Regular and Italic, 593 in Bold, 592 in Bold Italic) and its
  usMaxContext is computed again (6); post's isFixedPitch is 0, as the Devanagari is
  proportionally spaced (PANOSE, which classifies the Latin design, keeps
  Monospaced); head's bounding box and hhea's advanceWidthMax and extents are what
  the glyphs make them (yMin -530 in Regular, -547 in Bold; advanceWidthMax 1323 and
  1447). The line metrics, the em and every existing advance are the Hebrew stage's.
- nameID 0 gains Noto's copyright line and nameID 7 its trademark notice, "Noto is a
  trademark of Google Inc.", after the other two (OFL-FAQ 1.1-update7, Q3.7). New
  name records name the axis, STAT's values and Noto's stylistic set.

What stays the Hebrew stage's, measured on the files before the merge writes (it
refuses otherwise): every existing glyph's bytes as stored, its advance and side
bearing, every cmap entry, every lookup compiled alone, the features every existing
language system names, every glyph's GDEF class, the seven mark filtering sets, and
every name record but 0 and 7. GSUB is written around the Hebrew stage's own bytes:
the block of its lookups and every subtable they name is copied whole, so stock
JetBrains Mono's lookups are its bytes, under a ScriptList, FeatureList and
LookupList written again with the features sorted by tag, as OpenType's common table
formats ask. Ten LangSys tables and four FeatureParams of the old lists lie inside
that block, and stay there, named by nothing: 772 bytes in each style. GPOS is laid
out again by fontTools, as the Hebrew build lays it out.

What it shapes as, with hb-shape 14.5.1 on the 399 lines of
baud-font/tests/shape/devanagari.txt: Regular as the pinned static Noto Sans
Devanagari Regular, glyph for glyph under one renaming of glyph IDs, with the same
clusters, offsets and advances but the space's (the face's own, 600); Regular as
the variable font at wdth 62.5 and 81.25 too, and Italic at 62.5. Bold differs from
Noto's own static Bold in 7 of the 399 lines, in two glyphs' positions or advances
by one unit, and 7 of the 783 glyphs' boxes and 2 advances differ from that file's
by one unit: the rounding of an instance at wght 700. At wdth 62.5 Bold is within 2
units of the variable font read at wght 700 and wdth 62.5 (328 of the 399 lines
differ, in 69 glyphs), for the same reason. OTS 9.3.0 passes all four.


THE ITALICS

Italic and bold italic Hebrew lean right, with JetBrains Mono Italic's Latin and
digits, since 2026-10-24. Each is its upright design
(Regular, or Bold) sheared 9 degrees about y = 315, JetBrains Mono Italic's angle
(italicAngle = 9 in every master of its source), with the points moved by the same
shear so each stays on its letter, then fitted back into the cell where the shear
took a point out of it, meteg included (note AC 4b). The vowel points stay sheared,
as JetBrains Mono's own combining dots are.

The letters' rounds then take JetBrains Mono Italic's own correction
(build/optical.py), read from its sources (sources/JetBrainsMono.glyphs and
JetBrainsMono-Italic.glyphs at cd5227b). Its italic is its upright sheared, with two
departures at the rounds. First, where a bowl's outer curve met its straight side
farther from the corner than the inner curve across the stroke, the italic levels the
joints on o, O and q: o's and O's outer joints move level with the inner ones (o by
12-13 units, O by 10) and q's inner joints move in by 10, the same at acute and obtuse
corners (g and e keep their gaps). At the obtuse corner the shear thinned, the italic
has about 3% more ink than its sheared upright. Hebrew's bowls stop short by 6.6-31.2 units (Italic) and 23.2-45.5
(Bold Italic), so each outer joint moves toward its inner one by the gap, at most 13
units, JetBrains Mono's largest move: samekh, tet, shin and mem in Italic.ttf,
samekh, shin and mem in BoldItalic.ttf. Second, at a round corner the shear made
acute it shortens the curve's handles and at one it made obtuse it lengthens them, by
an amount that depends on what meets the corner; each Hebrew corner gets the amount
JetBrains Mono gives a corner like it. Against the plain shear the two move 12 glyphs
in Italic.ttf and 14 in BoldItalic.ttf, and the cell fit moves two point anchors.
Stems keep their horizontal width, as JetBrains Mono's italic keeps them.

Each merges into JetBrains Mono's own Italic or Bold Italic, under this family's name:
its family and style records are those the stock italics renamed into the family
carried, nameIDs 0 and 7 are as in Regular.ttf and Bold.ttf, and nameID 3 is this
style's own build tag (WHAT CHANGED).


BAUD'S OWN GLYPHS

What the Hebrew repertoire needs beyond Cascadia's letters and the redrawn points is
made by baud (build/cover.py) -- drawn, or built from this font's own letters -- and
taken from no other font, the rule since 2026-10-07: baud's own glyphs, aligned
with JetBrains Mono. Each is appended after every other glyph, so no
glyph ID moves, and is drawn upright; the italics take it through the letters' shear
and correction of rounds.

- U+20AA NEW SHEQEL SIGN: two interlocking hooks, a full stem, an arch and an inner
  stem each, the right hook the left turned half a turn about the cell's centre. 630
  units tall, the Hebrew letters' height, as faces that carry Hebrew draw it (Arial's
  and Courier New's stand at their alef's height); ink 30 to 570; stems 82 units in
  Regular and 104 in Bold, a little lighter than a letter's 90 and 125 so four stems
  and three gaps fit the cell, and arch crowns 78 and 100; shoulders 0.72 of the arch's
  half-width, squarer than a circle, as JetBrains Mono's n and the built het.
- U+05C6 NUN HAFUKHA: the built nun reversed about the cell's centre line. The
  Unicode Standard (9.1): "it often has the appearance of a rotated or reversed nun";
  the Hebrew chart's reference glyph is reversed. No point anchors: it is punctuation,
  and its dot above is U+0307, JetBrains Mono's own mark, which this font does not
  place on it (KNOWN DEFECTS).
- U+05F0, U+05F1, U+05F2, the Yiddish double vav, vav yod and double yod: two built
  letters side by side, the first on the right, each squeezed across to 252.5 units
  (a vav to 0.86 of its width, a yod to 0.70) and thickened back to the letters'
  stem weight, 45 apart within ink 25 to 575; full height kept.
- U+05EF YOD TRIANGLE: three built yods at 0.70 scale, weight restored, two side by
  side under one between them, 30 apart, the top yod at the letters' 630.
- The ligatures and the yod triangle take the points by the same anchor search as
  the letters, so a point under one sits at the font's below-point height, as under
  yod.
- U+FB1D-U+FB4F, the presentation forms. The 34 with a canonical decomposition are
  TrueType composites of it -- the letter and its points at exactly the offsets the
  mark-to-base lookup gives them -- so each draws as HarfBuzz draws its decomposition
  typed apart; each form is a base in that lookup with its letter's anchors, so a
  further point lands as on the letter. GSUB is not touched: Microsoft's Hebrew
  specification offers ccmp to compose or decompose them, but HarfBuzz composes a
  letter and point into a form only for a face with no mark feature, which this
  face has. U+FB1E VARIKA, "a glyphic variant of rafe" (Unicode 9.1), is a mark: a
  crescent opening upward, rafe's width, as thick as rafe's bar at its foot, at
  every letter's rafe anchor. U+FB20, the alternative ayin, is the ayin, which has no
  descender here. U+FB21-U+FB28, the wide letters, are their letters stretched across
  the cell to ink 20 to 580, stems eroded back to their weight, as Courier New, a
  monospace face, stretches its wide alef across its cell. U+FB29, the alternative
  plus sign, is JetBrains Mono's own plus of the style without its lower arm, lowered
  so its bar lies at the plus's foot, as Arial, Times New Roman and Courier New set
  theirs. U+FB4F, the alef lamed ligature, is the built alef with the built lamed's
  mast set on its upper-left stroke, rising to 794.
- U+0591-U+05AF, the cantillation marks, drawn after the Hebrew chart's reference
  glyphs from this font's own dot and strokes of 0.85 its points' bar (the winding
  ones -- shalshelet, zarqa, zinor, darga -- 0.6, so their turns stay open), 1.6 dots
  tall and 1.8 wide, as faces that carry them draw them two to three of their dots
  tall (Arial Unicode MS, Courier New, Times New Roman, SF Hebrew, Arial Hebrew). A
  mark below sits where hiriq does; one above at the letter's upper dot, at the
  height of the points above, as those faces set theirs at their shin dot's height;
  the postpositive pashta, telisha qetana and zinor at the holam's place, and left of
  a holam there; the prepositive geresh muqdam and telisha gedola at the shin dot's
  place. A mark below stands left of the point below before it, 30 units from its
  ink, tops aligned, and yetiv and dehi right of it: "the point always comes first
  (on the right) and the mark after it (on the left), except for the marks yetiv ...
  and dehi" (Unicode 9.1).

WHERE THE HEBREW COMES FROM AND WHAT WAS DONE TO IT

The source is Cascadia Mono from Cascadia Code v2407.24 (Microsoft; Hebrew designer
Liron Lavi Turkenich), taken as its variable font. Regular starts from the wght 400
instance and Bold from wght 700, the axis maximum and the closest to JetBrains Mono
Bold's stems. The steps (note AC 4a and 4b):

- The letters are re-weighted by erosion and dilation to JetBrains Mono's strokes:
  90 vertical and 82 horizontal in Regular, 125 and 113 in Bold.
- They are scaled to a 630-unit letter height and centred in the 600-unit cell. Of
  the 27 letters, 17 top out at 630, 9 at 631-640, and lamed's mast at 794 in Regular
  and 797 in Bold.
- The descenders of qof and the final forms are shortened to JetBrains Mono's -180.
- Tav's counter is widened.
- Every vowel point is redrawn on JetBrains Mono's own dot and placed by a clearance
  search against the letter. The Regular dot is 116; the Bold dot is 120 x 98 and the
  Bold dagesh 104.
- Meteg attaches to the vowel before it, and stays in the cell: where the vowel's own
  place leaves no room, the vowel and meteg move in that cluster alone
  (build/meteg.py).
- Two-row points (sheva, the hatafs) drop under ג ט ל ס ש so their top dot does not
  touch the letter.
- Dalet's leg stands at its roof's right end, the roof passing it by 45 units where
  round 1's passed it by 84, so that ד does not read as ז (CHANGELOG, 2026-10-24).
- Zayin's head is 300 units wide (Bold 314), narrowed from the left, so that dalet's
  roof is 1.39 times it, where round 1 left it 1.10 times (Cascadia's own design has
  1.37) (CHANGELOG, 2026-10-28).
- Italic and Bold Italic: the upright design leaning right 9 degrees, its rounds
  corrected as JetBrains Mono Italic corrects its own (THE ITALICS).

Nothing from Cascadia's Latin is used.


INTERIM PICKS
(settled where a text or a measurement decides it; interim otherwise)

- Devanagari: Noto Sans Devanagari's own glyphs, interim, until baud's own grid
  design replaces them. At Noto's scale (no scale applied): Noto's headline sits
  2.4% lower against JetBrains Mono's x-height than against its own Latin's (0.2 px
  at 14 px), and any other scale would round every point, advance and anchor. Its
  width axis carried as a variable axis, hidden from user interfaces. In the
  italics, upright: Noto draws no italic and nothing is synthesised; a slanted
  Devanagari is an open choice. In a Devanagari run, Noto's dotted circle under a
  mark with no base (THE DEVANAGARI).
- Dalet: H45, interim: the roof passes the leg by 45 units, the roof keeps its
  418 (Bold 438) width, also weighed with dalet's nearness to vav. No other
  sister pair is redrawn (NONE). Note AC 4d.
- Zayin: L300, interim: the head's left end moves 80 units right, so the head is
  300 wide (Bold 314) and passes the leg by 81 units on the left and 129 on the
  right (Bold 67 and 122). Under narrow-letter variant A, which keeps the other
  narrow letters as they were. Note AC 4e.
- Cantillation marks: drawn into the font, settled (note AD Q9). Their forms,
  variant B (larger, stronger, the winding ones finer), interim. Their
  placement, interim.
- Presentation forms: GSUB untouched (A), settled; the canonical forms as
  composites, interim; the alternative plus lowered, settled; the alef lamed
  ligature, settled; varika, settled. The wide letters stretched across the
  cell, interim, chosen with Courier New's measured.
- Nun hafukha: the built nun reversed, settled.
- Yiddish ligatures: B (each letter 252.5 wide, 45 apart), interim, against the
  first design's 230 and 60.
- Yod triangle: B (yods at 0.70, 30 apart), interim, against 0.62 and 40.
- Sheqel sign: shape B (squarer shoulders) at 630, interim, from renders, also
  weighed against a lighter variant (stems 74 and 92) and a redraw.
- Tree: NEW_PATH_A2, interim. Stock JetBrains Mono stays at
  baud-app/fonts/jetbrains-mono/ as the base. The Hebrew inputs live in
  baud-app/fonts/glyphs/languages/hebrew/ and the outputs here.
- Bold: BOLD_A_L2_AS_BUILT, settled. This is the gated Bold-A-L2 design, its
  bytes changed only by the two conformance fixes. That also answers 1d and 1e
  as built for now (both below).
- Regular: the gated Regular-A-L2 design, its bytes changed only by the two
  conformance fixes; the shown defaults for 1a (A) and 1b (L2). Interim.
- Italic and Bold Italic: lean right (2026-10-24). How far the rounds' handles
  are corrected is C_PER_KIND_FIT, settled: each corner takes JetBrains Mono's
  fit for corners of its kind. How far the bowls' joints are levelled is
  D13_LEVEL_CAPPED, settled: by the gap, at most 13 units. Their family name is
  the built family's (N_SHARE, settled).
- Inputs: COMMIT_VF, settled. Cascadia's variable font is committed with SOURCE
  and its licence, and the build instances it.
- Name bytes: KEEP_FINAL3_BYTES, settled, superseded for two fields on
  2026-10-08. That pick was made without the dropped trademark notice in its
  premise. Weighed again with both gaps and the standards' text, a departure
  from the standard or the licence is a defect, so both were fixed now
  (CHANGELOG). The rest of the name table
  (the prototype family name, nameID 3's tag) stays until decision 11, and is renamed
  once then.

other use is prohibited." and then gives OFL-based terms. The files here carry
Microsoft's copyright line in nameID 0 and JetBrains Mono's OFL sentence in nameID
13. They do not carry that text, and OFL.txt here carries both copyright lines over
the OFL 1.1. Answered on 2026-11-03: they ship under the OFL 1.1. The
CascadiaMono.ttf the build reads was checked that day byte for byte against
ttf/CascadiaMono.ttf of Microsoft's own release,
https://github.com/microsoft/cascadia-code/releases/download/v2407.24/CascadiaCode-2407.24.zip
(sha256 e67a68ee3386db63f48b9054bd196ea752bc6a4ebb4df35adce6733da50c8474; the font
08026f617eea315caca59898784e999f6cdb2d0c34d2040deeb042bcdc359fa2), and the repository's
LICENSE at the release's tag, v2407.24 (commit 56bcca3f), is OFL.txt byte for byte:

Name. Decision 11 in note AC is the family's name. It may not use the Reserved Font

nameIDs 8, 9, 11 and 12 stay until then; nameID 7 keeps both trademark notices.

Mono's italic corrects its rounds (THE ITALICS), the reading taken of the request for
italic Hebrew.


KNOWN DEFECTS
(From note AC 4a and 4b, except the first three and the last four -- meteg, the
letters its chain covers that no test checks, its joiner spellings, nun hafukha's
dot, a form followed by a point, two cantillation marks on one letter and a mark
beside a wide point -- which were measured on these files on 2026-10-08 or later and
are not in note AC. None of these is fixed by this build, except the meteg counts
the first item gives before and after 2026-10-26. Two earlier gaps, the missing hebr
script record and Cascadia's dropped trademark notice, were fixed; see the
CHANGELOG.)

- Meteg leaves the cell where the rule (build/meteg.py) finds no place inside it on
  the side its text order gives. Measured on these files with the round 3 critic's
  check (meteg 20 units clear of the vowel and the letter, its box inside x 0..600):
  <vowel, meteg> passes 274 of 297 clusters in Regular, 265 in Bold, 262 in Italic
  and 259 in Bold Italic (before 2026-10-26: 217, 172, 185 and 143); the rest are
  final letters, whose vowel stands beside the descender with no room for meteg to
  its left (in Bold Italic final nun and final tsadi with every vowel; Regular:
  final kaf and final pe with the hatafs and qubuts, final nun with those and tsere
  and segol, final tsadi with all but sheva and hiriq). <meteg, CGJ, vowel> passes
  291, 291, 293 and 291 (before: none, meteg drew on the vowel); the rest are qof
  with the wide vowels, whose vowel stands right of the leg with no room for meteg
  to its right. Meteg keeps its side there, clear of the letter, with the least ink
  outside the cell. There qof's meteg leaves the cell, measured with the round 3
  check, by 5 units with tsere and segol, 58 with the hatafs, 122 with qubuts and
  210 with hataf segol in Regular, 67-223 in Bold, 5-155 in Italic and 14-174 in
  Bold Italic (before 2026-10-26: 0, meteg drawn on the vowel). Right of a wide
  vowel on final kaf, final nun and final pe, meteg stands past the descender,
  inside the cell.
- Letters the chain covers that no test checks: U+05EF with meteg, CGJ and sheva,
  hataf segol or hiriq puts meteg 123-125 units out of the cell in Bold and 69-74 in
  Bold Italic, and in <vowel, meteg> with hataf segol or qubuts 42-50 in Bold and
  107-121 in Bold Italic (before 2026-10-26 out in every style, up to 143); U+FB47
  takes qof's anchors, and so qof's residual: 5-210 units in Regular, 67-223 in
  Bold, 5-155 in Italic and 14-174 in Bold Italic. Before 2026-10-26 both drew
  meteg on the vowel, inside the cell, in <meteg, CGJ, vowel>.
- Meteg's joiner spellings are open. <hataf, ZWJ, meteg> is the medial spelling (the
  Unicode Standard, 9.1); HarfBuzz 14.5.1 leaves meteg unplaced there, at offset 0,
  while baud's shaper attaches meteg through the ZWJ, and since 2026-10-26 its chain
  also moves the vowel (Bold bet, hataf patah, ZWJ, meteg: baud 616/362 against
  HarfBuzz 600/0). <hataf, ZWNJ, meteg> places as the plain spelling in both
  shapers, but HarfBuzz splits the cluster and baud does not. Medial meteg is
  unbuilt: it needs GSUB.
- Rare combinations overflow the cell sideways: qof with hataf segol by 104 units,
  and final tsadi with qubuts by 140 (Regular; Bold unmeasured). With a meteg after
  it, a wide vowel on qof goes further, to leave meteg room between it and the leg
  (Regular: tsere and segol 3 units past the cell, the hatafs 56, qubuts 120, hataf
  segol 148; Bold up to 219). The vowel goes past the edge: the first readings put meteg
  left of the leg, on a figure for its
  overflow that was wrong (6-37 units in Bold, where it is 48, with the italics'
  107-150 and its failing the check left out), and the measured figures put the
  vowel past the edge. Since
  2026-10-26, in <meteg, CGJ, vowel> on final kaf, and in Regular and Bold on final
  pe, the vowel leaves the cell on the left, up to 142-144 units in Regular (hataf
  segol; qubuts 116), 217-219 in Bold, 134 in Italic and 217 in Bold Italic; and in
  Bold Italic, with <hataf segol, meteg>, the vowel goes 8 units past the cell on
  every plain letter but qof (28 clusters a side carry more vowel overflow than
  before, against 4-12 in the other styles).
- After the digit 1, an unattached holam vanishes into it, as JetBrains Mono's own
  U+0307 does (Regular; Bold unmeasured).
- Bold at 26 px: the sheva's two dots fuse (dot-to-dot 124) and the lower dot is cut
  (0.40). At 28 px the reading is letter gap 16, dots 35, cut 0.16, and at 38 px it is
  clean.
- Regular at 26 px touches under the dipping letters (135) and fuses its sheva's dots
  (109).
- At 20 px the cut went from clean to faint in the bold styles, against the round
  before.
- The narrow letters were judged by non-native readers from baud renders, so they are
  open to a native reader's check (1a).
- A dot above nun hafukha, which the Unicode Standard spells U+0307 (9.1), is
  JetBrains Mono's combining dot, and no lookup places it on U+05C6: it sits where
  the pen leaves it, offset 0 in HarfBuzz 14.5.1, as on nun.
- A presentation form followed by a further point (FB31 then qamats, FB2E then
  meteg) is kept whole and the point placed by the form's anchors, where HarfBuzz
  decomposes the cluster; whether the two draw alike is unmeasured.
- Two cantillation marks on one letter overlap: two above take the same place (bet
  with revia and zaqef qatan, both at x offset 600 in HarfBuzz 14.5.1), and a second

  -120..56, and yetiv beside qamats out to 662.
