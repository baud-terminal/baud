FONTLOG for baud's embedded font: baud-app/fonts/built/

This is the SIL Open Font License's FONTLOG for the modified fonts in this directory:
what they are, what changed against what they came from, what was chosen in the
meantime, and what is still wrong. The licence is OFL.txt and the authors are listed in
AUTHORS.txt. MANIFEST records how the files are built and pins every input and output.

The record of the design work is note AC, docs/research/notes/ac-built-font.md: section
4a covers the Regular, 4b the other styles and the italic lean, 4c the italics as built,
and 6 the open decisions.

On macOS, scripts/bundle-macos.sh puts this directory's OFL.txt, FONTLOG.txt,
AUTHORS.txt and MANIFEST, and glyphs/languages/hebrew/'s OFL.txt and SOURCE, in the app
bundle beside the other embedded fonts' licences. Nothing packages baud for Linux yet; a
Linux package must carry the same files.


THE FILES

Regular.ttf     JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular plus Hebrew. The variant is A-L2.
Bold.ttf        JetBrains Mono 2.304 Bold plus Hebrew. The variant is A-L2.
Italic.ttf      JetBrains Mono 2.304 Italic plus Hebrew, leaning right. The variant is A-L2.
BoldItalic.ttf  JetBrains Mono 2.304 Bold Italic plus Hebrew, leaning right. The variant
                is A-L2.

All four are built by scripts/build-fonts.sh from baud-app/fonts/jetbrains-mono/ and
baud-app/fonts/glyphs/languages/hebrew/ (see SOURCE there). The script refuses to
write bytes other than these sha256s:

  Regular.ttf     769a5d99505247e42c8cfd0d013f68a1ad1cf5f4c64ccd96d73f1a3387c881bb
  Bold.ttf        13ce890885c189eeb3c21bef5cca32f4f96c362383fb1ecc7a428154f5575fe8
  Italic.ttf      c6b0e8a3375541de332ab95249471c5da8dcc0e4c5d801e8bbd2079e813712be
  BoldItalic.ttf  183877058f5ee024254897522bee85066f5b795350f7bba72e90ca9a45f503f1

Regular and Bold are the gated final3 files (note AC 4b; ea07610d... and c508ee5a...) with the two
conformance fixes in the CHANGELOG and, since 2026-10-24, one redrawn letter, dalet
(CHANGELOG), and nothing else. Before the dalet: take the 6-byte hebr script record
out of GSUB and of GPOS and both tables were final3's byte for byte, and every other
table but name (nameID 7) and head (its checksum) was final3's. The dalet changes that
glyph's outline, its anchors in GPOS and head's checksum alone. The gates were run
again on these bytes on 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-24 (CHANGELOG).

Running scripts/build-fonts.sh --check rebuilds all four and compares them with MANIFEST.
scripts/build-fonts.sh --italics rebuilds the two italics with the rounds' correction
off (both its parts) and requires the plain shear's earlier gated bytes (note AC 4b),
so the correction is the only thing the italics add to the shear.


WHAT CHANGED AGAINST STOCK JETBRAINS MONO 2.304
(each file against the stock file of its own style, measured with fontTools 4.63.0)

- 52 code points added, all in U+05B0-U+05F4:
  - the 27 letters, U+05D0-U+05EA;
  - the points: the vowels (nikud), dagesh, meteg, rafe, the shin and sin dots, the
    upper and lower dots and qamats qatan (U+05B0-U+05BD, U+05BF, U+05C1, U+05C2,
    U+05C4, U+05C5, U+05C7);
  - maqaf, paseq, sof pasuq, geresh and gershayim.
  U+05C6 and the Yiddish ligatures U+05F0-U+05F2 are not mapped. The glyph count goes
  from 1743 to 1795, and the stock glyph order is an exact prefix, so every existing
  glyph keeps its ID.
- No stock glyph changed: 0 glyf records, 0 advances and 0 cmap entries changed or
  lost. GSUB is byte-identical but for one added script record, hebr (below): with it
  taken out, every byte is stock's. The 20 combining points advance 0, and the 27
  letters and 5 punctuation marks advance 600 (one cell).
- GPOS goes from 5 lookups to 7. The 5 stock lookups are unchanged. The two new ones
  are mark-to-base (type 4), for the points on the letters, and mark-to-mark (type 6),
  for meteg on the vowel before it. They are appended to the existing mark and mkmk
  features. GDEF classes the 52 new glyphs (32 base, 20 mark; every stock glyph keeps
  its class) and gains a fourth mark filtering set, which only the new mark-to-mark
  lookup uses.
- GSUB and GPOS each gain a hebr script record, between DFLT and latn, naming DFLT's
  Script table: the same default language system and feature indices (in GPOS, mark
  and mkmk). Microsoft's 'Developing OpenType Fonts for Hebrew Script' registers hebr,
  and HarfBuzz's Hebrew shaper applies GPOS only when the script it chose is hebr.
  baud selects latn first, so what baud draws does not change.
- OS/2 sets the Hebrew Unicode range bit (ulUnicodeRange1 bit 11) and the Hebrew code
  page bit (ulCodePageRange1 bit 5).
- In the name table, the family is "Proto Hebrew Mono C", and the same in Italic.ttf
  and BoldItalic.ttf (below). nameID 3 carries the
  prototype tag. nameID 0 carries JetBrains' copyright line followed by Microsoft's,
  with the Reserved Font Name Cascadia Code. nameID 7 carries both trademark notices,
  each as its source font has it: "JetBrains Mono is a trademark of JetBrains s.r.o.
  Cascadia Code is a trademark of the Microsoft group of companies." (OFL-FAQ
  1.1-update7, Q3.7: "Any trademark notices must remain in any derivative fonts").
  nameIDs 8, 9, 11 and 12 are still JetBrains' manufacturer, designers and URLs, and
  nameID 13 is still JetBrains Mono's OFL sentence. baud draws nothing from the name
  table; since 2026-10-20 it reads the family to answer a program that asks which
  font is in use (xterm's OSC 50), and the answer is this family. The family name
  is an open call; see below.


THE ITALICS

Italic and bold italic Hebrew lean right, with JetBrains Mono Italic's Latin and
digits: the owner's call of 2026-10-24 ("learn right R"). Each is its upright design
(Regular, or Bold) sheared 9 degrees about y = 315, JetBrains Mono Italic's angle
(italicAngle = 9 in every master of its source), with the points moved by the same
shear so each stays on its letter, then fitted back into the cell where the shear
took a point out of it, meteg included (note AC 4b). The vowel points stay sheared,
as JetBrains Mono's own combining dots are.

The letters' rounds then take JetBrains Mono Italic's own correction
(build/optical.py), read from its sources (sources/JetBrainsMono.glyphs and
JetBrainsMono-Italic.glyphs at cd5227b). Its italic is its upright sheared, with two
departures at the rounds. First, where a bowl's outer curve met its straight side
farther from the corner than the inner curve across the stroke, the italic levels the
joints on o, O and q: o's and O's outer joints move level with the inner ones (o by
12-13 units, O by 10) and q's inner joints move in by 10, the same at acute and obtuse
corners (g and e keep their gaps). At the obtuse corner the shear thinned, the italic
has about 3% more ink than its sheared upright. Hebrew's bowls stop short by 6.6-31.2 units (Italic) and 23.2-45.5
(Bold Italic), so each outer joint moves toward its inner one by the gap, at most 13
units, JetBrains Mono's largest move: samekh, tet, shin and mem in Italic.ttf,
samekh, shin and mem in BoldItalic.ttf. Second, at a round corner the shear made
acute it shortens the curve's handles and at one it made obtuse it lengthens them, by
an amount that depends on what meets the corner; each Hebrew corner gets the amount
JetBrains Mono gives a corner like it. Against the plain shear the two move 12 glyphs
in Italic.ttf and 14 in BoldItalic.ttf, and the cell fit moves two point anchors.
Stems keep their horizontal width, as JetBrains Mono's italic keeps them.

Each merges into JetBrains Mono's own Italic or Bold Italic, under this family's name:
its family and style records are those the stock italics renamed into the family
carried, nameIDs 0 and 7 are as in Regular.ttf and Bold.ttf, and nameID 3 is this
style's own build tag (WHAT CHANGED).


WHERE THE HEBREW COMES FROM AND WHAT WAS DONE TO IT

The source is Cascadia Mono from Cascadia Code v2407.24 (Microsoft; Hebrew designer
Liron Lavi Turkenich), taken as its variable font. Regular starts from the wght 400
instance and Bold from wght 700, the axis maximum and the closest to JetBrains Mono
Bold's stems. The steps (note AC 4a and 4b):

- The letters are re-weighted by erosion and dilation to JetBrains Mono's strokes:
  90 vertical and 82 horizontal in Regular, 125 and 113 in Bold.
- They are scaled to a 630-unit letter height and centred in the 600-unit cell. Of
  the 27 letters, 17 top out at 630, 9 at 631-640, and lamed's mast at 794 in Regular
  and 797 in Bold.
- The descenders of qof and the final forms are shortened to JetBrains Mono's -180.
- Tav's counter is widened.
- Every vowel point is redrawn on JetBrains Mono's own dot and placed by a clearance
  search against the letter. The Regular dot is 116; the Bold dot is 120 x 98 and the
  Bold dagesh 104.
- Meteg attaches to the vowel before it.
- Two-row points (sheva, the hatafs) drop under ג ט ל ס ש so their top dot does not
  touch the letter.
- Dalet's leg stands at its roof's right end, where Cascadia's and round 1's stood
  near the middle, so that ד does not read as ז (CHANGELOG, 2026-10-24).
- Italic and Bold Italic: the upright design leaning right 9 degrees, its rounds
  corrected as JetBrains Mono Italic corrects its own (THE ITALICS).

Nothing from Cascadia's Latin is used.

Licence. Cascadia's repository LICENSE is plain OFL 1.1 under Microsoft's copyright
line with the Reserved Font Name. The release binary's own nameID 13 is a different
text: it opens "Microsoft supplied font. You may use this font to create, display,
and print content as permitted by the license terms or terms of use, of the
Microsoft product, service, or content in which this font was included. ... Any
other use is prohibited." and then gives OFL-based terms. The files here carry
Microsoft's copyright line in nameID 0 and JetBrains Mono's OFL sentence in nameID
13. They do not carry that text, and OFL.txt here carries both copyright lines over
the OFL 1.1. Whether that is enough to ship is the owner's call.

Also unasked: whether the build modules under glyphs/languages/hebrew/build/ and
casanchors.py beside them are Font Software under the OFL or baud code under the
repository's licence. anchors-400.json and anchors-700.json are data read out of
Cascadia Mono; hebrew/SOURCE states them under OFL.txt, as the font is, and the
licence call should confirm it.

The repository's LICENSE is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (Cargo.toml: license = "CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0").
OFL condition 5: "The Font Software, modified or unmodified, in part or in whole, must
be distributed entirely under this license, and must not be distributed under any
other license." So LICENSE carves the fonts out, in its section "Files this licence
does not cover": the four built styles with this file, OFL.txt and AUTHORS.txt
beside them; CascadiaMono.ttf and the two anchors files; and the stock fonts. It
names the two questions above as open and assigns the build modules to neither
licence. baud-font/tests/licences.rs fails if a font in the tree is neither baud's
own nor named there with its licence beside it.

Name. Decision 11 in note AC is the family's name. It may not use the Reserved Font
Name Cascadia Code, nor either of its words (SIL's OFL-FAQ 1.1-update7, Q5.4: "You may
not use individual words from the Reserved Font Names"), and it should not be
"JetBrains Mono", which is JetBrains' trademark. The prototype name and JetBrains'
nameIDs 8, 9, 11 and 12 stay until then; nameID 7 keeps both trademark notices.

Shear or optical italic. Answered: the italics are optically corrected as JetBrains
Mono's italic corrects its rounds (THE ITALICS), the reading the session recorded of
the owner's words "i do think we want italic in hebrew".


KNOWN DEFECTS
(From note AC 4a and 4b, except the last three -- meteg with CGJ, the unmapped code
points and U+20AA -- and the Bold meteg item's detail, which were measured on these
files on 2026-10-08 and are not in note AC. None of these is fixed by this build. Two
earlier gaps, the missing hebr script record and Cascadia's dropped trademark notice,
were fixed; see the CHANGELOG.)

- Meteg fails on 80 of 297 clusters in Regular. With hataf segol it sits 20 units out
  of the cell and with qubuts 6, and on final letters it leaves the cell. On qof it
  lands on the leg.
- Meteg fails on 125 of 297 clusters in Bold (it passes 172; note AC 4b). On these
  bytes, with HarfBuzz 12.3.0 under latn, the lookups baud selects (baud's own shaper
  gives the same on bet with hataf patah and hataf segol): on the 22 letters without a
  descender, meteg sits up to 60 units out of the cell with hataf segol, 42 with
  qubuts, and 10 with hataf patah and hataf qamats, which Regular keeps inside; on the
  five with one, qof and four final forms, 32 of 55 clusters leave the cell.
- Meteg in the italics passes 185 of 297 clusters in Italic and 143 in Bold Italic,
  where Regular passes 217 and Bold 172: the right lean's points sit left of the
  upright's, and the cell fit moves the vowel but not meteg alone (note AC 4b).
  Measured on these files with the round 3 critic's check; the plain shear's are the
  same counts.
- Rare combinations overflow the cell sideways: qof with hataf segol by 104 units,
  and final tsadi with qubuts by 140 (Regular; Bold unmeasured).
- After the digit 1, an unattached holam vanishes into it, as JetBrains Mono's own
  U+0307 does (Regular; Bold unmeasured).
- Bold at 26 px: the sheva's two dots fuse (dot-to-dot 124) and the lower dot is cut
  (0.40). At 28 px the reading is letter gap 16, dots 35, cut 0.16, and at 38 px it is
  clean.
- Regular at 26 px touches under the dipping letters (135) and fuses its sheva's dots
  (109).
- At 20 px the cut went from clean to faint in the bold styles, against the round
  before.
- The narrow letters were judged by non-native readers from baud renders, so they are
  the owner's to check (1a).
- A meteg spelled to sit right of its vowel, as Unicode 17 section 9.1 gives it --
  <meteg, CGJ, vowel> -- draws on the vowel. Measured in baud's shaper: in Regular,
  bet + meteg + CGJ + sheva puts meteg at x 259..341, y -186..-36, over the sheva at
  x 242..358, y -275..-26 (Bold: 244..357 over 240..360), and hataf patah is the
  same. Meteg lands exactly where bet + meteg alone puts it, on the letter's anchor:
  no vowel precedes it for the mark-to-mark lookup. HarfBuzz 12.3.0 under latn gives
  the same boxes. The left spellings, <vowel, meteg> and <hataf, ZWNJ, meteg>, come
  out left of the vowel, and the ZWNJ moves nothing: in all 297 letter-vowel clusters
  of both styles, meteg lands where it does without it (HarfBuzz 12.3.0 under latn;
  baud's shaper on bet), so the meteg items above apply to both spellings. In Bold,
  bet + hataf patah + ZWNJ + meteg puts meteg at x -10..103, 10 units past the cell's
  left edge, and with hataf segol at -60..53 (Regular: 26..108, inside; with hataf
  segol -20..62, 20 out).
- Bold's and Bold Italic's zayin head ends slant 22 units, where Regular's are
  upright: round 1 widened zayin's head by moving the points above y 540 (design.py
  EDITS), and Bold's head is thicker, its underside at 517, so only the top corners
  moved. Measured on these files; not changed by the 2026-10-24 build.
- U+05C6, the cantillation marks U+0591-U+05AF and the presentation forms
  U+FB1D-U+FB4F are not mapped.
- U+20AA NEW SHEQEL SIGN is not mapped, though the code page 1255 bit is set (it is
  cp1255's 0xA4) and Cascadia Mono maps it. No embedded face maps it. On macOS the
  default stack draws it from Menlo (measured by cmap); on Linux it comes from DejaVu
  Sans Mono if that face maps it (unmeasured); with no system face it draws as a box.

Not the font's defects, but what a reader sees:

- baud has no bidi, so Hebrew draws in typed order.
- baud-core's cluster store drops vowel points in longer pointed text (note AC,
  decision 16).
